## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Does AI-generated ad copy actually outperform human-written copy in A/B tests?

In my experience, not on its own. Raw AI copy wins about as often as it loses.

I found this out in March 2024 on a Meta campaign for a home-services client. I ran 40 ChatGPT-written headlines against our three best human-written ones. Spend was roughly $11,000 over nine days. The AI batch produced exactly two winners. That's a modest hit rate, and it's roughly what you'd expect from any large batch of fresh ideas, human or machine.

So does that mean AI copywriting tools are useless? No. It means the value is somewhere other than where most people look for it.

The advantage wasn't that the model wrote better headlines than a skilled copywriter. It was that it let us test 40 variations instead of three or four. Most teams never test that broadly because writing that many credible variants takes too much time. With AI, the cost of generating options drops to nearly zero, and the real work shifts to testing.

Here's the practical approach I'd recommend:

1. Generate a large batch of variants across different angles, not just different wordings of the same idea.
2. Test them against your current best human copy as the control.
3. Take the winners, identify why they worked, and feed that insight back in to generate a second round.
4. Repeat. The lift comes from iteration, not from any single output.

If you skip the testing and simply publish whatever the tool gives you, you'll get average results faster. If you pair it with a structured testing process, it becomes a powerful idea engine.
