**Title:** Is anyone else finding that more AI ad variants actually makes your testing worse?

I keep seeing teams make this mistake, and I made it myself. They use Midjourney or a similar tool to make hundreds of ad variants, put them all in one ad set and let Meta sort it out.

Last March a skincare brand I work with generated 212 variants in one afternoon. We loaded 60 of them into a single ad set. Eleven days and about $14k later, three ads had taken roughly 91% of spend. Most of the other 57 never got enough impressions to tell us anything. We hadn't tested anything. We'd just paid the algorithm to pick its favorites early.

What fixed it was doing things in the opposite order. First we tested a small number of concepts that were actually different from each other: a different angle, a different promise, a different pain point. Once one concept won, we used the AI tool to make lots of versions of that winner, changing the background, crop, color and copy.

AI is great at making versions of an idea and pretty bad at deciding which idea is worth making. If your variants don't differ in concept, the extra volume mostly just spreads your budget thin.

How do others set this up? Do you split concepts into separate ad sets, or use ABO for the concept round?

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What is the best way to test AI-generated ad creative variations on Meta?

Short answer: test concepts first, then use AI to make versions of the concept that wins. Most teams do it the other way around, and that is the main reason more AI creative so often fails to improve results.

Here is what it looks like when it goes wrong. In March 2025 a DTC skincare client of mine generated 212 Midjourney variants in one afternoon. We put 60 of them in a single Meta ad set. After eleven days and roughly $14,000, three ads had taken about 91% of spend. The other 57 barely got any delivery, so we learned almost nothing from them. Meta's delivery system commits to early favorites quickly. When you give it 60 near-identical options, it is mostly choosing between noise.

A better structure:

1. Define concepts, not variants. A concept is a distinct angle: a different problem, promise, proof point or audience. Three to five is plenty for a first round.

2. Give each concept a fair read. Keep them separate so one cannot starve the others. Before you judge, set a spend or conversion threshold and pick your success metric, such as cost per purchase rather than CTR alone.

3. Make versions of the winner. This is where AI tools are strongest. Once you know the angle works, generate variations in background, framing, color, model, headline and format.

4. Retire and refresh. When the winner fatigues, go back to step one with new concepts instead of endlessly reworking the visuals of the old one.

The core idea is that AI makes new versions nearly free, but it does not tell you which message your buyer cares about. You still need a disciplined concept test for that.
