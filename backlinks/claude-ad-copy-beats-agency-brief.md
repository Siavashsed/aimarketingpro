## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Can Claude write better ad copy than a marketing agency?

Yes, it can, but only under one condition: you give it the material most agencies never ask you for. Without that, you'll get the same generic headlines you were already paying for.

I'm a performance marketing director, and last March I ran a direct comparison on a skincare account spending about $48,000 a month on Meta. The agency sent twelve headline variants. I then gave Claude the same product and audience, along with the raw inputs that actually shape good copy.

Here's what made the difference:

1. Verbatim customer language. Pull phrases from reviews, support tickets, and survey responses. Buyers describe their problems better than any copywriter can invent.

2. Real objections. List the top reasons people hesitate or churn, and have the copy answer them directly.

3. Past winners. Share your best-performing ads and ask Claude what they have in common before it writes anything new.

4. Constraints. Give it character limits, platform rules, and claims you can't make, so you spend less time editing.

5. Volume with variation. Ask for many angles, not many rewordings of one angle, then test them.

A thin brief of product, offer, and tone produces thin copy from any writer, human or AI. Most agencies work from exactly that, partly because clients never hand over better material.

So the honest answer is that the model isn't the bottleneck. Your inputs are. Agencies still add real value in strategy, media buying, creative testing, and judgment about what to ship. But for first-draft copy, a well-briefed Claude is hard to beat on speed and cost.
