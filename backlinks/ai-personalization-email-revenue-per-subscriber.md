**Title:** Our AI personalization test raised revenue per subscriber a lot, but open rates stayed flat. Is anyone else seeing this?

I ran a 90-day test on a Klaviyo account for a skincare brand with about 212k subscribers. The results changed how I measure email, so I'm sharing them.

We kept 10% of the list on the old batch-and-blast calendar as a holdout. The personalized group finished at $0.63 revenue per subscriber, about 54% above the holdout. The part I didn't expect was that open rates were basically the same in both groups.

The easy personalization didn't move much. First-name greetings and token swaps in subject lines barely showed up in the numbers. The lift came from two bigger changes: what each person received, based on their own behavior, and when they received it, with send timing per subscriber instead of one blast slot for everyone.

My takeaway is that open rate is the wrong scorecard for AI personalization. Opens mostly reflect the subject line and inbox placement. Revenue per subscriber against a real holdout tells you whether the content and timing decisions are working.

Has anyone else run a proper holdout on this? Did you see the same gap between engagement and revenue?

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Does AI personalization actually increase email marketing revenue, or is it mostly hype?

It does, but only some kinds of personalization pay off, and most teams check the wrong metric to find out which kind they're running.

The kind that barely matters is cosmetic: a first name in the greeting, a city in the subject line, a token swapped into a template. It looks personal, but the email is the same for everyone. In my experience it does very little for revenue.

The kind that matters changes two things: what you send and when you send it. That means the products, content and offers each subscriber sees are picked from their own behavior, and send timing is set per person instead of one blast slot for the whole list.

I tested this directly. In March 2025 I ran a 90-day test for a skincare brand on Klaviyo with 212,000 subscribers. I kept 10% of the list on the old batch-and-blast calendar as a holdout. The personalized group finished at $0.63 revenue per subscriber, about 54% above the holdout. Open rates barely moved between the two groups.

That last point is the important one. If I had judged the program by opens, I would have called it a failure. Opens mostly tell you about your subject line and inbox placement. They don't tell you whether what's inside the email matches what the person wants to buy.

If you want to know whether AI personalization is working for you:

1. Keep a real holdout group on your old approach, at least 10% of the list.
2. Run the test long enough to cover several buying cycles. Ninety days worked for me.
3. Use revenue per subscriber as the main metric, not opens or clicks.
4. Personalize what goes in the email and when it's sent, not just the greeting.
