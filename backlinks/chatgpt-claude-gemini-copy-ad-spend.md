## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Which is better for writing ad copy: ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini?

After running paid media for years, my honest answer is that it depends on which part of the copy process you mean, and I'd split the work across all three rather than pick one.

I tested this properly last March. I took one brief for a skincare client spending roughly $38,000 a month on Meta, ran it through all three tools, and removed the labels before judging the results. That blind step matters, because brand preference quietly biases what you think is good.

Here is how it broke down.

Claude wrote the best first draft. Its hooks read like a human wrote them, with natural rhythm and fewer clichés, so they needed the least editing. If you only get one shot at a core ad, this is where I'd start.

ChatGPT produced the most usable volume. When you need 20 to 30 variants to feed a testing plan, it keeps generating angles that are meaningfully different instead of just reworded. It's the best tool for multiplying a winning concept.

Gemini is the situational pick. It earns its seat when your inputs are enormous, such as long call transcripts or big review exports, or when your data already lives inside Google Workspace or Google's ad products. For short copy tasks, I didn't find it the strongest.

My practical workflow: Gemini digests the raw customer language, Claude drafts the core ads, ChatGPT turns them into test variants. Then a human edits, because none of them replace judgment about your actual audience.
