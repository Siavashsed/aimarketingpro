**Title:** Anyone else tried fully automating social with AI and watched engagement drop? Here's what happened when I did it for 21 days

Last March I ran an experiment for a skincare client. I set up a Claude and Buffer pipeline to handle their Instagram and LinkedIn for 21 days. Roughly 63 posts, and I made zero human edits.

The posts were fine. Grammatically clean, on brand, consistently scheduled. And engagement still slid from 3.1% down to about 1%. Nothing was technically wrong, it just read like nobody was home.

What I took from it: scheduling, resizing, first drafts and reporting are all things I'm happy to hand to a machine now. What doesn't transfer is the moment someone with an actual point of view decides what to say. The comments that used to come in from real customers dried up first, which told me the audience could tell.

I'm not anti-automation. I still use AI for most of the grunt work. But the pipeline now ends with a person who has an opinion, not a publish button.

Curious where others have landed. Have you found a line between what's safe to automate and what needs a human? Did anyone see the same kind of slow fade rather than an obvious failure?

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Can you fully automate social media management with AI without hurting engagement?

Short answer: you can automate most of it, but not all of it, and the part you can't automate is the part that drives results.

I'm a performance marketing director and I consult on AI marketing, and I tested this directly. Last March I let a Claude and Buffer pipeline run a skincare client's Instagram and LinkedIn for 21 days. That was roughly 63 posts with zero human edits. Engagement rate fell from 3.1% to about 1%. Nothing was broken. The posts were polished, consistent and on brand. They just had no point of view.

Here's how I now split the work.

Safe to automate:
- Scheduling and publishing
- Resizing and reformatting assets for each platform
- First drafts and caption variations
- Reporting and performance summaries
- Repurposing a long piece into snippets

Keep human:
- The opinion or angle of each post
- Replies to comments and DMs
- Anything tied to a current event, a customer story or a result you actually saw
- Final approval before publishing

The reason is simple. Audiences follow accounts because someone seems to be thinking. A model can imitate tone very well, but it can't have a stake in what it says. When every post is a plausible average of what's already out there, people stop reacting, and the algorithm follows them.

A practical approach: let AI produce three drafts, then have someone with real experience pick one, rewrite the first line in their own voice and add one specific detail only they would know. That takes five minutes per post and keeps nearly all the time savings.

I wrote up the full 21-day experiment, including what I'd change, here if you want more detail: https://siavashsed.github.io/aimarketingpro/automating-social-media-ai-authenticity/

Use AI as the production team, not the editor-in-chief.
