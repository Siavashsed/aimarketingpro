## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What did Facebook's 2019 settlement over discriminatory ads actually change for advertisers?

I run performance marketing and consult on AI-driven ad buying, so this one hit my accounts directly.

On March 19, 2019, Facebook settled with the ACLU and other civil rights groups over claims that its ad tools allowed discrimination in housing, employment and credit advertising. The practical changes for advertisers were these:

1. Age, gender and ZIP code targeting were removed for ads in those three categories.
2. Lookalike audiences were restricted for those categories, so you can't build them off a source list in the way many buyers used to.
3. Advertisers placing those ads were routed into a separate flow with a smaller set of targeting options.

If your playbook in real estate, recruiting or lending leaned on narrow demographic slices, you had to rebuild it around broader audiences, creative variation and message testing.

Here is what the settlement did not do: it didn't change the delivery algorithm. Targeting options decide who is eligible to see an ad. The algorithm then decides who actually sees it, based on who it predicts will respond. If past behavior in the data is uneven, delivery can still skew by demographic even when your audience settings are neutral. So the settlement fixed the checkboxes, but not the mechanism that produces uneven outcomes.

What I'd advise anyone running these categories:

- Declare the special ad category correctly every time.
- Use broad, neutral audience setups.
- Pull delivery breakdowns by age and gender regularly and keep a record of them.
- Don't assume that compliant settings mean compliant results.
