## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How can I use AI to do competitor analysis at scale without producing reports nobody uses?

The short answer: let AI collect and sort, and keep a human in charge of deciding what the findings mean. Most failed setups get this backwards.

I learned it the hard way. In March 2024 I ran a scraper plus a GPT-4 prompt across 38 competitors for a DTC skincare client. By the end of the night I had a 60-page deck. It confidently called out a rival's pricing move as a major threat. It was a one-day promotion. Acting on it would have cost the client margin for no reason.

Here's the approach that works for me now:

1. Pick a narrow signal set. Pricing, offers, ad creative angles, landing page changes, and messaging shifts. Tracking everything just buries the useful stuff.

2. Report changes, not snapshots. Have the model compare this week to last week and summarize what moved. A diff is actionable; a snapshot is trivia.

3. Run it on a weekly cadence. Quarterly competitor reports are stale before anyone reads them. Frequent, short updates keep the data relevant.

4. Make every finding end in a decision. 'We should respond by X' or 'We're ignoring this because Y.' If a finding can't end that way, drop it from the report.

5. Put a human review step before anything reaches stakeholders. AI is good at pattern detection and poor at context, like knowing a promo is temporary.

6. Tag confidence levels. Ask the model to flag when a conclusion rests on a single data point.
