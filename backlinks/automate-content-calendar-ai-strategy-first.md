## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How much of a content calendar can you safely automate with AI?

From my experience running performance marketing and consulting on AI workflows, the honest answer is about 70%, as long as you're clear about which 70%.

The part that's safe to automate is logistics. That means scheduling posts, formatting one piece of content for each platform, repurposing a long article or video into short snippets, and producing first drafts. These tasks are repetitive, the output is easy to check, and a mistake is cheap to fix.

The part I would not automate is the thinking. That includes positioning (why this brand says this, to this audience, now), the angle of each piece, and the final read before publishing. A model can write fluent copy, but it doesn't know that your client ended a promotion yesterday, that legal flagged a claim last week, or that a competitor just launched something that changes your message.

I learned this the hard way. In March 2024 I connected Zapier and ChatGPT late on a Sunday night to fill 60 posts for a DTC skincare client. A few days later, several of them were still promoting a sale that had ended. Nothing was broken technically. I had simply handed decisions to a tool that was only meant to handle logistics.

My approach now:

1. Write down what you will not automate before you build anything.
2. Automate in batches small enough that a person can actually read every post.
3. Check anything time-sensitive, such as offers, dates and pricing, against a live source.
4. Keep a named human responsible for the final approval.

If you want a longer walkthrough of how I split the work, I wrote it up here: https://siavashsed.github.io/aimarketingpro/automate-content-calendar-ai-strategy-first/

The short version is that AI is great at filling a calendar and poor at deciding what belongs in it. Keep those two jobs separate and you get the speed without the cleanup.
